# FretLab audit — September 2026

Second audit. The first drove `docs/REMEDIATION-PLAN.md` (Stages 1–4) and the
`docs/DESIGN-PASS.md` list came out of using the app rather than reading it.
Stages 1–3 and the design pass are complete; Stage 4 is 2 of 5.

This one is written after that work, against the tree at commit `201065f`, and
is deliberately critical: the useful half of an audit is the part that says
what is still wrong. Measurements are from the tree, not from commit messages —
the last audit note in `CLAUDE.md` claimed fixes that had not landed, which is
the failure mode this one tries to avoid.

**§10 is the one that stays open, and it needs you rather than me.** Detection
is verified to better than 0.01 cents against synthesised tones. Microphone
gain, room noise and the harmonics of a real string are precisely what
synthesised input does not exercise, and no amount of test-writing closes that
gap. Play a string at `/tuner` and it is settled either way.

**§7 is improved rather than closed.** Blues, harmonic minor and melodic minor
are not modes of anything already tabled and still need hand-authored
positions. The other content gaps — minor keys, a larger chord library, more
songs — are FL-26's scope and remain open.

Three of the fixes turned up defects of their own, all recorded in their
commits: the accessibility checks found meter buttons that announce as “3” and
“4” with no indication they set the time signature; the line-length guard found
three more blob files than the audit's threshold had caught; and the first dead
CSS sweep produced 23 rules with no selector, which builds in dev and fails the
production minifier.

## Summary

**The music is right, and that is the hard part.** Theory, fingering, scale
positions, interval patterns, pitch detection and synthesis are all checked
against independent references rather than against themselves, and several are
checked against each other. That foundation is genuinely good and unusual.

**The product is thin on top of it.** 21 drills exist and only 4 can be scored,
so the practice loop, the progress screen and the new review scheduler all rest
on a quarter of the content. This is now the single biggest constraint on the
app being useful, and it is a data problem rather than a hard one.

**The test suite is strong where the code is pure and absent where it is not.**
Every React hook in the codebase is untested — and both runtime crashes reported
from the browser during this work were in that layer.

|---|---|
| Source | ~14,900 lines TS/TSX across 83 files |
| Stylesheet | **8,586 lines** — larger than any source area |
| Tests | 3,968 lines, 633 assertions, 221 tests |
| Passing | 208, with **13 skipped and not reported as such** |

## What is working well

### The musical foundation is verified, not asserted

This is the part worth protecting. Content that is hand-written is
machine-checked against theory, and the strongest checks are *independent* —
they compare two things that were derived separately and must agree:

- A major pentatonic box must be the same physical shape as the next minor
  pentatonic box up, because a major pentatonic is its relative minor's. Two
  separately typed tables agreeing is what catches a plausible typo in either.
- Chord qualities (`Imaj7 ii7 iii7 IVmaj7 V7 vi7 viiø7`) are **computed** from
  the intervals each cell contains and then asserted, not just asserted.
- The tuner and the fretboard must agree about what an open string is, in
  standard tuning and in Drop D.
- The synthesiser is tested by the app's own pitch detector — FL-15 listening to
  FL-22 — which caught a 15-cent drift no ear would have flagged on a demo tone.

### Defects are verified by being reintroduced

Nearly every guard added since Stage 2 was checked by breaking the code it
protects and watching the test fail. A test that has never failed is not known
to test anything, and this codebase mostly knows.

### The "no invented numbers" rule held under pressure

It is the rule the first audit found broken most often, and it now shapes real
decisions rather than being decoration: the runner records a null accuracy
because it measures none; the review scheduler refuses to grade a timed session
for the same reason; `Progress` shows an empty state rather than a zero.

### Boundaries and purity are enforced by tools, not habit

`eslint-plugin-boundaries` is real and its own test writes a deliberate
violation to prove the rule fires. The `react-hooks` lint has shaped the code
into three reusable patterns for external state rather than a scattering of
effects.

## What needs improvement

### 1. Only 4 of 21 drills can be scored · **Severity: High**

`Train` is the only screen that records an accuracy, and it can only run the 4
`TRAINING_MODULES`. The other 21 drills are timed and record `accuracy: null`.

Everything downstream inherits that ceiling:

- `Progress`'s accuracy ring and daily-accuracy chart describe 4 drills.
- FL-23's review scheduler can only schedule what it can grade, so "Today's
  review: 12 items" is only reachable by practising the same 4 drills across
  several keys.
- A learner who works entirely through routines and drills — the app's own
  primary path — generates **no accuracy data at all**.

This is not a hard problem. `Train`'s scoring mechanism (tap the targets, count
hits and misses against the shape) applies to any drill with a shape, which is
most of them. It is the highest-value work available.

### 2. The React layer is untested · **Severity: High**

Thirteen modules are never imported by a test, and they include every hook:
`useClock`, `useElapsed`, `useMetronome`, `useStoredKey`, `useTuner`,
`lastRun`, `hooks.ts`, `audio.ts`.

Both crashes reported from the browser during this work were in that layer, and
both traced through `useClock`:

- a hydration mismatch, because the wall clock was read during render;
- `RangeError: Invalid time value`, because a stale module made `now` arrive as
  `undefined` and the guard tested `=== null`.

Pure logic is covered to 633 assertions. The layer that has actually broken
twice in production has none.

### 3. Screen coverage greps source instead of rendering · **Severity: Medium**

`rendered-html.test.mjs` holds 139 assertions, of which 31 read source files and
match regexes against them. That pins implementation details rather than
behaviour, and it has already cost twice during this work: one assertion was
pinned to `--board-natural` and another to `${low}`, and both failed when the
code improved rather than when it broke.

Nine screens have no test of any kind: `DrillDetail`, `GroupedDrills`,
`KeyDetail`, `Keys`, `RoutineDetail`, `Routines`, `ScaleDetail`, `SignIn`,
`Summary`.

### 4. `npm test` reports green while skipping 13 tests · **Severity: Medium**

The integration suite skips silently without a live server, so the headline
"208 passing" hides 13 that did not run. The handoff warns about this in prose;
the command does not. A suite that is green when a third of its API surface was
never exercised trains people to trust the wrong number.

Related: when a dev server *is* running, those tests contend on the shared local
D1 and one fails roughly one run in five.

### 5. The stylesheet is 8,586 lines and 5% dead · **Severity: Medium**

Larger than any source area, and larger than the entire test suite. Sixteen
class names — 56 rule blocks, roughly 428 lines — are referenced nowhere in any
`.tsx`: `fretboard-legend`, `accuracy-list`, `heat-map`, `heat-labels`,
`routine-keys`, `routine-bands`, `root-dot`, `drill-fingering`, `finger-guide`,
`key-wheel-trigger`, the four `tab-*` classes, `tab-theory-link`,
`desktop-side-progress`.

Several are residue from screens deleted during this work — `accuracy-list` was
`Summary`'s fabricated per-drill percentages — which means deletions have not
been finishing the job.

### 6. Two files are written as single-line JSX blobs · **Severity: Low**

`Onboarding.tsx` has a 1,578-character line; `ChordPlayingLesson.tsx` has a
1,058-character one. Both are unreviewable in a diff, and both came in from a
different tool's house style. They are not wrong, but nobody can see a change to
them.

### 7. No error boundary anywhere · **Severity: Medium**

There is no `error.tsx`, no `global-error`, no `componentDidCatch`. The two
reported crashes each took out the whole screen, and the second produced
`RangeError: Invalid time value` with no recovery path. A single route-level
boundary would have turned both into a contained message.

### 8. Content gaps that limit the teaching · **Severity: Medium**

- **Position tables cover 3 of 11 scales.** The other 8 use a sliding window,
  labelled honestly, but a learner opening Dorian gets meaningfully less than
  one opening Major.
- **Major keys only.** `FIFTHS` is 12 major keys; there is no minor-key mode,
  which FL-26 covers.
- **16 chords, 5 qualities.** No maj7, no 9ths, no slash chords.
- **6 songs**, which is a demo set rather than a library.

### 9. Accessibility beyond contrast is unmeasured · **Severity: Medium**

Contrast is now guarded to WCAG AA with a test. Nothing else is. There are 101
`<button>` elements and 37 `aria-label`s, 15 `role`s and 2 `aria-live` regions.
FL-16 added keyboard navigation to the fretboard grid, but no test asserts a tab
order, a focus trap in the key modal, or that every control has an accessible
name. There are no `<img>` tags, so the missing `alt` count is a non-issue.

### 10. The tuner has never met a guitar · **Severity: Low**

Detection is verified to better than 0.01 cents against synthesised tones, which
is a different claim from FL-15's "it tunes a real guitar". Microphone gain,
room noise and string harmonics are exactly what synthesised input does not
exercise.

## Things deliberately left alone

Recorded so the next reader does not re-litigate them:

- **Three scale positions carry no finger numbers.** Correct: minor pentatonic
  box 3 in A runs frets 9–13 but the hand anchors at 10 and reaches back to 9,
  so mechanical numbering would be *wrong* rather than merely absent.
- **The full neck still scrolls on a phone**, inside a windowed view. Stacking
  it across two rows was rejected: a fretboard split into two rows of six
  strings reads as twelve strings, and loses the spatial correspondence that
  makes the diagram work at all.
- **`playTones` survives alongside `playShape`** for screens showing a scale or
  an interval, which have no strings to take pitches from.

## Priorities

1. **Score more than 4 drills** (§1). Unblocks progress, review and the whole
   retention half of Stage 4. Highest value in the codebase.
2. **Test the hooks** (§2). The layer that has broken twice has no coverage.
3. **Make `npm test` honest about skips** (§4). One line; stops the suite
   training people to trust a wrong number.
4. **Add a route-level error boundary** (§7). Small, and turns a white screen
   into a message.
5. **Sweep the dead CSS** (§5), then FL-24–26 content work.
